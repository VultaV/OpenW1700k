# r62 MLO matrix and bridge-offload control — 2026-11-02

## Outcome

All four requested AP combinations were measured on the Mac, using 90-second, four-stream TCP downlink and uplink trials. **No 5→2.4 GHz or 6→2.4 GHz data-link transition was observed.** The Mac had to remain at its current position; its initial 6 GHz RSSI was -12 dBm. Lowering AP TX power at this range does not establish that a weak-link handoff threshold was reached.

The installed r62 candidate still exhibits long TCP receive stalls, particularly in configurations including 2.4 GHz. A same-association, fixed-power ON/OFF/ON bridge-offload comparison reduced but did not eliminate zero-throughput intervals when offload was disabled. This is a lead for further investigation, not a confirmed root cause or repair.

## Attenuation protocol

Each trial had six 15-second stages:

- 5 GHz target: auto → 15 → 8 → 3 → 1 dBm → auto.
- 6 GHz target: auto → 12 → 6 → 3 → 1 dBm → auto.
- 2.4+5 targeted 5 GHz; 2.4+6 targeted 6 GHz; the triple targeted both higher bands; 5+6 targeted 6 GHz.
- The actual reported per-radio TX power was captured after each change.
- Two setup attempts were excluded before any matrix measurements: a 5 GHz beacon startup failure, and a readiness/client-identity issue. The latter used a privacy-masked Mac address and initially treated a link undergoing DFS CAC as ready. Identity was subsequently obtained from the router's neighbor entry, and hostapd's active-link list became a readiness requirement.

AP TX attenuation changes downlink conditions. It does not directly attenuate the Mac's uplink or necessarily produce the AP-side RSSI inputs used by the selection policy. No claim of a successful weak-signal fallback can be made from this near-range protocol.

## Matrix results

Mbps below is the receiving endpoint's mean. Zero counts are **iperf's approximately one-second intervals reporting 0 Mbps**, not proof that the radio sent absolutely no frames.

| AP configuration | Observed client links | Data primary | Down Mbps | Down zero intervals | Up Mbps | Up zero intervals |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2.4 + 5 GHz | 0x0003 | 1 | 573.6 | 58 | 1707.7 | 0 |
| 2.4 + 6 GHz | 0x0005 | 0 | 72.9 | 69 | 971.8 | 3 |
| 2.4 + 5 + 6 GHz | 0x0007 | 2 | 207.9 | 25 | 1756.3 | 0 |
| 5 + 6 GHz | 0x0006 | 2 | 1348.0 | 0 | 1350.1 | 0 |

Link IDs: 0 = 2.4 GHz, 1 = 5 GHz, 2 = 6 GHz.

- No data-primary change occurred during any trial.
- In 2.4+5, AP transmit completion increments were only on link 1.
- In 2.4+6, they were only on link 0. This association started with a 2.4 GHz data primary; it cannot be counted as a tested 6→2.4 GHz transition.
- In triple-link and 5+6 downlink trials, they were only on link 2.
- Therefore an active 0x7 client mask did not mean that its download was aggregated across three bands.
- AP transmit completion counters do not establish the data band used for the Mac's uplink.
- The first 2.4+5 downlink collapse began around 25 seconds, whereas power changes were at 15 and 30 seconds. Do not attribute the collapse to one power-setting command merely by proximity.
- No target-client `AP-STA-DISCONNECTED` event was found inside any measurement window. Configuration/rejoin events outside those windows are excluded. No new MCU timeout, PCIe completion timeout, reset-failure, warning or call-trace line was found in the post-test kernel query. These observations do not identify the stall's source.

## Fixed-power bridge-offload ON/OFF/ON control

A new 2.4+6 association selected data primary 2. Its links and primary remained unchanged during all three trials. TX power stayed at auto; no attenuation commands were issued. Only the owned bridge-flow-offload table was toggled using its existing apply script; the global firewall and Airoha NPU were not restarted or disabled.

| Bridge offload | Down Mbps | Zero intervals | Minimum interval Mbps | Ping packet loss |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ON #1 | 1014.3 | 36 | 0.0 | 0.2% |
| OFF | 917.9 | 2 | 0.0 | 0.0% |
| ON #2 | 118.5 | 82 | 0.0 | 0.0% |

Independent server-port snapshots frequently showed four `HW_OFFLOAD` entries in both ON conditions and consistently zero in OFF. Counts were not always four: the last ON trial also reached zero while the bridge table remained enabled. The port-only filter can include leftover entries, so it is not a per-packet attribution of every stream. Table enablement must not be equated with continuous hardware forwarding throughout a stall. Ping remained responsive even when TCP reception stalled. The very different ON repetitions show substantial variability; this is one ordered comparison, not a randomized repeated experiment.

This supports prioritizing the bridge fast path/NPU/PS interaction for further correlation. It does **not** establish a specific PPE/NPU defect, exclude the client or firmware, or show that disabling bridge offload solves the problem. Wireless NPU handling remained active in the OFF condition, and two zero intervals remained.

## Restoration and wired protection

- Wireless and bridge-offload files exactly match their pre-test backups by SHA256 and `cmp`; both UCI pending-change lists are empty.
- Original AP layout restored: separate 2.4 GHz channel 1 at 20 MHz/23 dBm, MLO 5 GHz channel 36 at 160 MHz/23 dBm and 6 GHz channel 37 at 160 MHz/18 dBm; TX power auto.
- Bridge offload restored to enabled for the original ports.
- Independent rollback processes were armed before changing configuration. The final rollback guardian was cancelled **after** both configurations and runtime offload state were verified, preventing a delayed extra Wi-Fi reload.
- The control controller returned a nonzero exit during its first restoration attempt: the apply script ran before the recreated bridge port was forwarding. The configuration copies had succeeded. Runtime offload subsequently recovered through the existing hook; manual final verification confirmed the hashes, no pending deltas, active AP links and enabled state. The original controller error is retained in `control.log`; it is not silently counted as a successful script exit.
- Final Wi-Fi-bound 3-second, 10 Mbps reverse TCP connectivity check received approximately 10.1 Mbps.
- Independent wired observations: **902 checks, 0 link failures, 0 router ping failures**. This is sampled evidence, not proof of uninterrupted delivery of every wired packet.
- Matrix ping processes were terminated without final aggregate summaries; no exact aggregate loss percentage is claimed for those eight trials. Raw replies/timeouts remain available. The three control trials were stopped with SIGINT and include packet-loss summaries.

## What remains unverified

- Actual weak-signal 5→2.4 GHz and 6→2.4 GHz fallback, recovery to the higher link, and seamless AP link removal/reconfiguration.
- A causal explanation for the intermittent TCP stalls or the earlier all-band authorization/November chip-stop incidents.
- Air sleep/wake behavior, long-term stability and repeated/randomized offload controls.

No firmware was built, installed or published during these measurements. No new release version was created. r60–r62 remain unpublished candidates in the working checkout. Further source/release work must follow `AGENTS.md` and reconcile build provenance.
